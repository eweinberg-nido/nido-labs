Semana Uno

Around 40 students transfer into our school every year and most of them land in the middle of a term. Semana Uno is a card that fits behind a phone case plus a WhatsApp group run by Grade 11 mentors. It covers what isn't in the school handbook: how to get a Bip! card without a RUT yet, which micro actually goes past the gate, where to eat that isn't the casino, and what to do when your Chilean plan runs out of data.

Student's justification · proposed 4.0

Every visual decision on this card has a number behind it. I tested three sizes with 9 new students in October and the phone-case size got picked up 8 times out of 9. The lanyard version got picked up twice, and two people said it "looked like a school thing."

Student's justification · proposed 3.5

Our welcome letter is 900 words and 700 of them are things the school needs you to know: uniform, bus, insurance, the app you have to download. The last paragraph says "please don't hesitate to reach out."

What the structure is actually saying is that support is information, and that asking is your job. I put 3.5 because I think the reading itself is 4.0 work but I only did it to one text. Doing the same to the website and the tour would be the whole thing.

Mentor script + group activity log.gdoc

The silent group, the explanations she considered first, and the change that altered it.
Student's justification · proposed 4.0

The feedback I got in November was "the group is dead." That's the kind of feedback you can act on badly. My first three ideas were all card ideas: make the QR code bigger, add the group link to the back, put a sticker on it. All of them assumed people weren't joining.

Then I looked at the log. 14 people had joined. They just weren't talking. The feedback said the group was dead and the actual problem was the opposite of what that sounded like.

What people were telling me underneath it was that being the first to ask something in a group of strangers is worse than staying lost. So the fix isn't on the card at all. A Grade 11 mentor posts a question in the first week and answers it themselves, so the first message is never a new student's. Messages went from 2 in three weeks to 9 in the first week after.

The same thing happened with Sr. Rojas telling me the icons were noisy. He meant the line weights. What was actually wrong was that I had eleven icons at that point, and fixing the weights let me see that three of them were saying the same thing.

Self-assessment · 10 Dec
4.0written rationale

In both cases the useful thing was underneath what people actually said, and I have the message counts before and after to show that the change worked.

Advisor verification · confirmed 4.0 · 12 Dec

The 4.0 descriptor asks for feedback to be examined closely enough that the important insight within it is recognised. This is the clearest example of that I have read in the cohort. You record three responses you considered and set aside, then the explanation you arrived at, then a measured result. The three you set aside are what make the account credible.

The observation about the icons works in the same way. Sr. Rojas identified line weight, and correcting it allowed you to see a redundancy he had not mentioned.

To sustain this next semester: both examples here come from an adult or from data. Take a piece of feedback from a team member you disagree with, and examine it as carefully.

Results against the three criteria set in October, the adoption email from Admissions, the Semester 2 mentor rota, and a section on what did not work.
Student's justification · proposed 3.5

In October we wrote down three things that would mean this worked, before we knew if it would. Most new students in the group. Someone other than us answering questions. Admissions handing it out without being asked.

Two of three. 19 of the 22 students who joined this term are in the group. Admissions has been putting the card in the enrolment envelope since 24 November. But the third one is still me. I'm the one who answers most nights, and two mentors have signed up for March but haven't done it yet.

What failed: the buddy lunch. We ran it twice. Three people came the first time, one the second, and the person who came alone told Ines it was worse than eating by herself. We cut it. I'd rather write that down than pretend the whole thing worked.

I put 3.5 because I have one term of numbers and no idea whether being in a WhatsApp group in September means anything by May. The real test is whether it runs in March without me, and that hasn't happened yet.

The 3.0 descriptor asks for impact to be evaluated against clear criteria supported by user data, and that is met here.
