The Conceptual Shift: From 10 Ones to "1 Ten"

In this pivotal lesson, 1st graders transition from thinking of 10 as simply ten individual counted objects ($1, 2, 3... 10$) to understanding **"10" as a single bundled unit** ("1 ten"). This foundational concept forms the bedrock for place value understanding, multi-digit addition, and composition of teen numbers as $10 + n = 1n$.

Target Standards

  • 1.NBT.B.2: Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones.
  • 1.NBT.B.2.a: 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones — called a "ten."
  • 1.NBT.B.2.b: The numbers 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 ones.

Essential Focus Question

"How does grouping 10 single items into '1 ten' make it easier to build, count, and explain numbers from 11 to 19?"

Target Shift: Counting by ones $\rightarrow$ Grouping by tens

Learning Progression

Prior: Counting to 20, filling 10-frames with single dots.
Current (L8): Grouping 10 cubes into 1 stick. $10 + 4 = 14$.
Next: Adding within 20 by making a ten ($8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3$).

UDL Mindset for L8

  • Multiple Models: Tower of 10, Double 10-frame, Rekenrek, Egg carton.
  • Multi-sensory Auditory: Rhythmic chants ("1 Ten and 3 ones... THIRTEEN!").
  • Flexible Expressive Options: Build, point, draw, clap, or speak.

Engagement (7.1) Autonomy & Relevance

Real-World Story Context Choices

Allow students to choose their favorite "packaging" theme for bundling 10 objects into 1 full unit:

  • Bakery Theme: 1 Box of 10 donuts + extra single donuts.
  • Toy Factory Theme: 1 Tower stick of 10 blocks + extra loose blocks.
  • Panda Sanctuary Theme: 1 Bundle of 10 bamboo sticks + extra single shoots.
Tag: MLL, Executive Function
Engagement (8.3) Collaborative Learning

Structured Partner Roles

When working in pairs to build numbers 11-19, assign clear rotating micro-roles to ensure equal active participation:

  • Partner A ("The Ten Builder"): Snaps 10 loose cubes into 1 solid stick of 10.
  • Partner B ("The Ones Collector"): Counts and places the extra 1-9 ones.
  • Both ("The Reporters"): Chant together: "1 Ten and [X] Ones makes [Number]!"
Tag: Peer Collaboration
Representation (2.5) CRA Progression

Dual-Color Tactile & Visual Bridge

Use strict color consistency across all physical and visual models to highlight the distinction between 10 and ones:

  • Blue Stick: Always represents the unified 1 Ten tower.
  • Yellow Loose Cubes: Always represent individual Ones.
  • Color-Coded Equations: Write 10 + 4 = 14.
Tag: Fine Motor, Visual
Representation (3.1) Auditory Processing

Rhythmic "1 Ten & Extra Ones" Auditory Chant

Reinforce language patterns for MLLs and auditory learners with rhythmic call-and-response during cube building:

Teacher: "What do we have?"
Class (stamping feet for 10): "1 TEN!"
Teacher: "And how many extra?"
Class (clapping ones): "And 5 ONES... FIFTEEN!"
Tag: MLL / Language Support
Action & Expression (4.1) Physical Response

"Ten-Clap & Finger-Flash" Movement Response

Provide kinesthetic alternatives to written or spoken answers during whole-group checks:

  • Show 1 Ten: Raise both hands overhead in an Overhead Circle ("1 Ten Dome").
  • Show Ones: Flash individual fingers in front of chest for the ones.
  • Allows non-verbal expression & active sensory input.
Tag: Motor, Kinesthetic
Action & Expression (5.1) Flexible Expression

3-Option Tiered Assessment Menu

Give students autonomy to demonstrate their understanding of "Ten as a Unit" using their preferred modality:

  • Option A (Tactile/Verbal): Build number with cubes and explain to teacher/peer.
  • Option B (Visual/Draw): Circle 10 dots on paper and color remaining ones.
  • Option C (Symbolic/Equation): Complete missing addend equations ($10 + ? = 17$).
Tag: Differentiated Assessment
Engagement (7.2) Curiosity & Play

"Teen Number Mystery Sack" Game

Place pre-made sticks of 10 and loose cubes inside a paper bag. Students reach in, feel 1 stick of 10 and loose cubes without looking, and predict the total teen number!

Tag: Tactile Mystery
Representation (3.2) Pattern Recognition

Quick-Flash Double 10-Frame Subitizing

Flash a full 10-frame + loose dots for 3 seconds. Train students to immediately say "10 and..." rather than recounting the top frame dot by dot.

Tag: Subitizing Skill
Action & Expression (6.1) Scaffolded Discourse

Bilingual & Picture Sentence Stems

Provide desktop reference cards with visual icon prompts:

"I have 1 ten and [ __ ] ones. My number is [ __ ]."
Tag: MLL Scaffold

Activity 1: Clare's Cubes - Structuring Teen Numbers (20 Mins)

UDL Focus: Choice Station Menu

In Clare's Cubes, students look at collections of cubes (e.g., 14 cubes) arranged either as 14 loose ones or 1 stick of 10 plus 4 loose ones. Adapt this task using 3 differentiated station options:

Station 1: Concrete

Tactile Cube Snap Station

Students take 14 loose unifix cubes. Teacher prompts: "Can you snap 10 together to make a single stick?" Compare how fast it is to count 1 stick + 4 ones vs. 14 loose cubes.

Station 2: Visual

Double 10-Frame Stamp Station

Students use stampers or dot markers on printed double ten-frames. They completely fill top 10-frame first, then stamp the remaining ones in the bottom frame.

Station 3: Story Context

Donut Box Packaging Station

Students act as bakers packing 10 donuts into 1 box container (egg carton cut to 10) and keeping extra donuts on a serving plate.
