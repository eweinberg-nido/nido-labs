Analysis
Written Text · Visual Text
▼ Learning progressions — Analysis
Strand 1.0 2.0 3.0 4.0
Analysis of written text Summarizes literal content without identifying underlying patterns, arguments, or connections between texts. Identifies explicit themes and connections between texts, though deeper subtext or implications remain unexplored. Analyzes how written elements interact to construct meaning, arguments, and implicit themes. Evaluates complex intertextual nuances, critiquing subtle bias, rhetoric, and subtext across mediums.
Analysis of visual text Describes what a visual shows without asking what it is doing or who made it. Identifies the choices a visual makes, such as colour, framing or omission, and names an obvious effect, without connecting them to purpose or audience. Analyses how visual choices construct meaning for a particular audience, including what has been left out. Critiques bias, rhetoric and framing across visual sources, comparing how different makers construct the same claim.
Half points: A student with a 3.5 can do what 3.0 describes consistently and some of what 4.0 describes. In this domain that usually means a reading that holds for one text but has not yet been extended across several.

Inquiry & Design Processes
Gathers multiple perspectives · Honors iterative process · Reflects on efficacy of process stage
▼ Learning progressions — Inquiry & Design Processes
Strand 1.0 2.0 3.0 4.0
Gathers multiple perspectives Relies on a single source or familiar viewpoint, ignoring diverse or conflicting perspectives. Identifies a few contrasting viewpoints, though representation remains surface-level or unbalanced. Actively seeks and integrates diverse, credible viewpoints to build a balanced understanding. Critically compares and synthesizes complex, divergent perspectives to refine inquiry and challenge assumptions.
Honors iterative process Completes a single draft or model with minimal effort to test, revise, or refine ideas. Makes superficial revisions when directed, treating iteration as a formal requirement rather than a learning tool. Embraces testing and critique to make meaningful, progressive improvements across multiple iterations. Drives continuous, self-directed refinement, treating setbacks as vital data points to innovate and transform design.
Reflects on efficacy of process stage Offers brief or superficial statements about actions taken without assessing their effectiveness. Identifies basic strengths and weaknesses of a process stage with teacher prompting. Evaluates the effectiveness of specific process decisions and identifies actionable takeaways for growth. Metacognitively evaluates process choices, anticipating challenges and optimizing methodologies for future contexts.
